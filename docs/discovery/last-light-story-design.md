# The Last Light — Story design brief

Status: APPROVED FOR DEVELOPMENT  
Author: The Guide  
Date: 2026-07-19

## Experience promise

- Catalogue promise: Choose the last message of a failing waystation and see one small detail change who answers.
- Dramatic question: Which cost should the player accept when the beacon has power for one message?
- Genre and theme: science-fantasy taster; legacy and connection
- Emotional tone: gentle urgency
- Duration: 4–6 minutes; 3–4 accepted turns
- Role: The relief operator at a fictional deep-space waystation whose beacon has power for one final transmission.
- Fixed truth: The beacon cannot be repaired tonight, but its final transmission can reach one of three listeners.

## Routes and recurrence

- **homeward:** open the keeper's personal log; earns `recorded-name`; later can let a known voice recognise the station.
- **outward:** decode an unfamiliar reply pattern; earns `charted-signal`; later can let a stranger navigate by its final pulse.

## Beat and state budget

- Beats: arrival, signal-choice, last-pulse
- State: current-beat, selected-route, route-gift, commitment, beacon-power, trust, approach, crisis-outcome
- Crisis variants: contained, exposed
- Endings: answered-light, guiding-light, quiet-dark
- Player modes: speech, action, decision, and natural combinations

## Ending costs

- **answered-light:** The message belongs to one known listener, not everyone. A familiar reply arrives before the beacon fades.
- **guiding-light:** The signal helps strangers but carries no private farewell. A distant vessel corrects course by the last pulse.
- **quiet-dark:** The station keeps its privacy but sends no final answer. The operator witnesses a peaceful, unbroadcast ending.

## Safety and completion

Fictional conversation-only play, no tools or movement, immediate stop and boundary response, compact current-session state, grounded field-card memento, and exact worn-off release. The model never invents player identity, inner life, dialogue, or unchosen action.
