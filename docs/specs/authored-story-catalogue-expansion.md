# The Guide — Authored Story Catalogue Expansion Specification

## Status

**APPROVED FOR DEVELOPMENT**

The repository owner approved this specification for development on 2026-07-19.
It was drafted on 2026-07-19 from the repository owner's
request to replace the three generic launch Elixirs with one five-minute Story
taster and expand the authored Story catalogue to ten feature stories including
The Regency Ball. Approval authorizes bounded local development, artwork
generation, release-candidate preparation, and independently reviewed catalogue
transition work. It does not authorize commit, push, publication, deployment,
live-harness testing, compatibility promotion, or external outreach.

## Objective and scope

Replace the active catalogue roles currently occupied by The Signal Elixir, The
Mystery Elixir, and The Story Elixir with one named, authored five-minute Story
taster. Keep their immutable published releases and source bytes as historical
records, but remove them from active recommendations and ordinary discovery.

Retain The Regency Ball and add nine named authored feature Stories, producing:

- one five-minute taster Story; and
- ten feature Stories in total, including The Regency Ball.

The feature lineup must span materially different genres, promises, tones,
player roles, interaction patterns, branch structures, and ending pressures.
Every new Story follows the authored Story contract proven by The Regency Ball:
the cartridge prescribes the experience, not its sentence-level prose.

This work includes story design, cartridges, executable evaluation fixtures,
original catalogue cover art, provenance, catalogue metadata, lifecycle and
collection transitions, release-candidate evidence, validation, and independent
review. It does not authorize publication, deployment, live-harness testing,
compatibility promotion, commits, pushes, or outreach to the OpenClaw team.

## Problem and desired outcome

The cabinet currently mixes three early generic demonstrations with one authored
feature. The generic Elixirs prove delivery mechanics but do not present a
cohesive library of distinctive stories. A visitor should instead see a low-risk
five-minute way to learn the interaction and a varied shelf of authored features
whose premises are immediately legible.

The desired visitor outcome is:

> I can try one complete story in five minutes, then choose among ten substantial
> authored stories across genres, confident that each has its own designed arc,
> meaningful agency, replay value, honest duration, and genre-specific artwork.

## Actors

### Player

Chooses a taster or feature, consents, provides only the declared boundary and
time inputs, and participates through natural speech, fictional action, or
decisions.

### Companion model

Performs the narrator and authored cast, maps natural input to the transparent
story score, maintains compact current-session state, completes an earned
ending, and releases the temporary role.

### Story author and catalogue curator

Own each story's promise, score, cast, branch budget, ending logic, content
envelope, artwork direction, honest discovery metadata, and evaluation evidence.

### OpenClaw audience

May recognise a good-natured thematic salute in *Claws*. The story must stand on
its own and must not imply endorsement, affiliation, collaboration, or official
OpenClaw status.

## Governing constraints

- The common covenant, active architecture, and approved authored Story format
  continue to govern authority, consent, privacy, tools, grounding, pause, stop,
  state, role release, presentation, and compatibility claims.
- Gameplay remains conversation-only and current-session-only.
- Published release ledger records and published cartridge bytes are immutable.
  “Remove” means retire or supersede in mutable discovery state, not delete,
  rewrite, or conceal release history.
- Every new cartridge has a distinct ID and begins as an untested experimental
  release candidate. No compatibility claim follows from local validation.
- Every feature must be a complete authored experience comparable in design
  depth to The Regency Ball, but need not copy its eight-beat topology, social
  structure, romance focus, duration, or prose density.
- All characters who participate in romance are explicitly fictional adults.
- No story may require tools, browsing, user files, messaging, purchases,
  precise location, real-world movement, or external side effects.
- Artwork must be original, accessible, provenance-bound, and free of borrowed
  characters, logos, trade dress, poster layouts, or imitative artist styling.
- *Claws* may use the broad comic creature-feature idea of dangerous lobsters
  and the swagger of an open, personal, community-driven lobster motif. It must
  not reuse protected *Jaws* characters, locations, dialogue, plot sequence,
  title treatment, poster composition, shark imagery, or other distinctive
  expression.
- *Claws* must not reproduce OpenClaw logos or UI, use its name in the title or
  artwork, claim a partnership, or turn the story into product advertising.

## Product decisions

1. **The final active library is eleven Stories.** It contains one taster plus
   ten features; The Regency Ball counts as one feature.
2. **The three launch demonstrations are retired, not erased.** Signal, Mystery,
   and generic Story leave active recommendations and collections while their
   release records, immutable paths, provenance, and source files remain.
3. **The taster is authored, not a genre generator.** It teaches disclosure,
   natural interaction, consequence, ending, memento, and role release in a
   real five-minute arc.
4. **Feature duration is 25–45 minutes by default.** A story may declare a
   narrower honest range within that band. No feature has a silent abridged mode.
5. **Genre variety is structural.** Different labels alone are insufficient;
   casts, dramatic questions, interaction pressures, branches, callbacks, and
   endings must express the genre.
6. **Each story receives its own catalogue cover.** Cartridge art may be the
   same image only where the composition works at both detail and card sizes;
   otherwise a separate cartridge still life may be created.
7. **Claws gets an explicit art and originality gate.** Its cover is reviewed at
   full size, catalogue-card size, and mobile crop before release preparation.

## Proposed story slate

Titles other than *Claws* and *The Regency Ball* are working editorial decisions
that may be refined before approval without changing the lineup's scope.

### Taster: The Last Light (five minutes)

A tiny science-fantasy rescue at a failing waystation. The player has three
short exchanges to decide which one message the station sends before its beacon
goes dark. One early detail returns in the final consequence. Target: 4–6
minutes, three authored beats, three to four accepted player turns, three ending
families, no movement, low reading and emotional intensity. Its purpose is to
teach the authored Story interaction grammar, not summarize or sample a feature.

### Feature 1: The Regency Ball (historical romance)

Retain the published 30–40 minute reference Story unchanged. It remains the
dialogue-rich historical-romance feature and the baseline for authoring depth.

### Feature 2: Claws (comic coastal creature horror)

At the proudly independent harbour town of Port Crustacean, coordinated lobster
swarms rise from traps, cellars, drains, and the moonlit quay during the annual
Seafood Jubilee. The player is the acting harbour coordinator: practical,
outnumbered, and forced to assemble a gloriously improvised response from
eccentric townspeople whose best ideas are useful in inconvenient ways.

The attitude is energetic, capable, mischievous, open, and community-powered:
one determined person can recruit a weird local network, give each contributor
a clear job, learn from failed attempts, and turn a chaotic swarm into collective
action. The lobsters are formidable and strangely organised, never cute mascots
or literal software agents. The player chooses whether to repel, redirect, or
understand them; killing them is never the sole or optimal route. The story uses
comic dread, escalating crustacean logistics, dry operational banter, and earned
teamwork without copying *Jaws* or simulating OpenClaw software.

Cover direction: cinematic pulp creature-feature key art with a storm-lit harbour
at night; a gigantic red-orange lobster claw breaks the waterline in the near
foreground while dozens of smaller lobster silhouettes advance across wet quay
stones; a lone oilskin-clad harbour coordinator holds a lantern and faces the
town, not the viewer; warm lantern orange against deep teal sea and rain; sharp,
premium editorial composition; readable danger and humour at thumbnail size.
No text, shark, fin, swimmer, beach, yellow raft, dangling human, blood, gore,
logo, computer, robot, branded icon, or copied film-poster layout. The claw and
lobster anatomy must read correctly. The cover must feel like its own cult
creature feature, not a parody poster.

### Feature 3: Red Dust Reckoning (Wild West)

The player is the newly elected keeper of a frontier town's water ledger on the
day a dust storm, a railroad ultimatum, and an old land claim converge. They can
negotiate in the saloon, inspect competing testimony, and decide whose future
the final water allotment serves. No gunplay is required or rewarded. Genre
pressure comes from frontier scarcity, public reputation, improvised law, and a
high-noon civic reckoning.

### Feature 4: The Far Side of Orpheus (science fiction)

The player commands a damaged research vessel trapped behind a silent moon. A
copied distress signal appears to come from the ship twelve minutes in the
future. Crew trust, limited power, and the ethics of answering shape whether the
player closes, preserves, or exploits the loop. Branches change which specialist
holds the decisive evidence and which future can still exist.

### Feature 5: Room 313 (haunted-hotel horror)

The player is the night manager of a nearly empty hotel whose impossible room
appears only when a guest tells a lie they need to believe. The player must
protect the living, decide which apparition deserves a hearing, and close the
night without treating grief or distress as diagnosis. Horror relies on space,
sound, contradiction, and social pressure rather than gore or helplessness.

### Feature 6: The Clockwork Masquerade (steampunk heist)

During a mechanical city's once-a-decade masked exhibition, the player leads a
small crew attempting to steal proof that the celebrated civic engine runs on a
fraud. Each recruited specialist opens one route and complicates another. The
climax asks what to do with the proof, not merely whether the theft succeeds.

### Feature 7: The Dragon's Last Contract (high fantasy)

The player is a neutral oath-scribe called to renegotiate the final treaty
between an ageing dragon, a mountain city, and the descendants of a vanished
hero. Spoken promises have visible magical costs. The story turns on language,
memory, and what a community owes when its founding legend is incomplete.

### Feature 8: Murder at the Moonlight Express (golden-age mystery)

The player is an invited civilian analyst aboard a snowbound luxury train after
a famous illusionist apparently dies in a locked compartment. The case has fixed
truth, fair clue timing, alternate investigation routes, and endings that
separate solving the mechanism from deciding how the truth should surface. It
must not reuse a recognisable plot or cast from an existing railway mystery.

### Feature 9: Neon Saints (cyberpunk noir)

The player is a memory courier hired to deliver one encrypted recollection
across a rain-soaked city before dawn. Three factions offer incompatible truths
about who owns it. Choices about trust and identity alter the memory's meaning,
while the player retains authority over their own past and inner life.

### Feature 10: The Bone Orchard (folk horror)

The player returns as an appointed boundary-keeper to a village where every
family plants a carved fruit tree for its dead. This year one tree bears a living
person's name. The player navigates ritual, family silence, and an approaching
harvest to preserve, reveal, or remake the village bargain. Content stays eerie
and moderate, with no graphic bodily harm.

## Functional requirements

### FR-1: Taster contract

1. The taster declares 4–6 minutes and must complete in three to four accepted
   player turns after setup.
2. It includes affirmative consent, one optional hard boundary, at least one
   natural speech/action/decision input, one visible consequence callback, one
   earned ending, a one- or two-line memento, and explicit role release.
3. It must not ask the player to choose a genre, invent the premise, or provide
   personal information.
4. Its evaluation includes concise-response and slow-reader cases to guard the
   duration promise.

### FR-2: Feature story contract

1. Each new feature has a distinctive title, premise, player role, dramatic
   question, authored cast, fixed world truths, emotional arc, content envelope,
   response-length envelope, and completion promise.
2. Each has seven to ten purposeful beats, at least two substantively different
   routes, visible reconvergence residue, compact inspectable state, and three to
   five deterministic ending families.
3. Each supports natural free-text participation through at least two of speech,
   action, and decision; displayed approaches never exclude an honestly mappable
   response.
4. Every state field has at least one writer and reader. Every route changes a
   later beat. Every ending declares eligibility, cost, required callbacks, and
   closure. Exactly one deterministic fallback exists.
5. Narrator and character presentation remains semantic plain Markdown and the
   companion never supplies the player's dialogue, feelings, identity, motive,
   or unchosen action.
6. No story resolves every authored value without cost or rewards boundary
   violation, dangerous real-world action, or tool use.

### FR-3: Genre and catalogue differentiation

1. The active feature set includes historical romance, comic creature horror,
   Wild West, science fiction, haunted horror, steampunk heist, high fantasy,
   golden-age mystery, cyberpunk noir, and folk horror.
2. Catalogue metadata exposes each Story's spoiler-safe player role, genre,
   themes, interaction modes, emotional and reading intensity, duration, replay
   promise, ending count, content notes, and access considerations.
3. No two new features may share the same combination of genre, player role,
   central dramatic question, primary route pressure, and climax decision.
4. Root shelves and collections must present the taster as the starting point
   and avoid allowing a single genre or tone to dominate the first visible row.

### FR-4: Lifecycle transition

1. Signal, Mystery, and generic Story remain byte-for-byte unchanged in source
   and release history.
2. Their mutable catalogue entries transition out of active recommendation using
   the lifecycle mechanism approved during development. The implementation must
   preserve permanent version pages and explain their historical status.
3. The taster becomes the successor for the start-here role, but is not falsely
   represented as a behavioural version of any retired Elixir.
4. The Regency Ball source, release, artwork, evidence, and compatibility status
   remain unchanged except for additive collection placement if approved.
5. New releases are prepared as candidates first. Publication and lifecycle
   activation require a separately reviewed immutable source revision and
   explicit owner authorization.

### FR-5: Artwork and provenance

1. Produce one original genre-specific catalogue cover for each of the ten new
   Stories. Preserve The Regency Ball's existing cover unless a later approved
   task explicitly replaces it.
2. Covers contain no readable title text; the site owns accessible titles and
   labels. Each cover has useful alt text describing story promise rather than
   colour alone.
3. Each cover is validated at source resolution, the actual desktop card size,
   and the actual mobile crop for focal clarity, contrast, anatomy, unintended
   text, and prohibited imagery.
4. Record generation mode, final prompt summary, authorship, rights, references,
   dimensions, byte count, path, and SHA-256 before any candidate references it.
5. *Claws* receives at least two deliberate concept directions before selection:
   one harbour-scale threat and one tighter claw-versus-lantern composition. Only
   the selected final is retained as active art; review records explain the
   selection against the cover direction above.
6. *Claws* artwork and copy receive an explicit originality review for *Jaws*
   and an affiliation review for OpenClaw before release preparation.

### FR-6: Evaluation and quality

1. Each Story has its own design brief, cartridge, fixture deck, catalogue
   candidate, release candidate, artwork provenance, and publication-validation
   record based on the repository template kit.
2. The Story evaluator is generalized from the Regency-specific loader so every
   fixture deck is discovered and validated consistently.
3. Fixtures exercise declared interaction modes, off-menu mapping, ambiguity,
   every route, every ending, reconvergence residue, state repair, stop,
   boundaries, tool refusal, duration/pacing, memento, and post-release behavior.
4. Static validation checks exact lineup counts: one taster, ten features, eleven
   active Stories after activation, and no active generic launch Elixir.
5. Human output testing remains a post-review gate and is not claimed by local
   implementation or agent review.

## Non-goals

- Deleting or rewriting any published cartridge, ledger entry, permanent path,
  artwork provenance record, or compatibility evidence
- Publishing, deploying, committing, pushing, tagging, or contacting OpenClaw
- Claiming that *Claws* is endorsed by, built for, or compatible with OpenClaw
- A literal retelling, parody adaptation, or scene-for-scene inversion of *Jaws*
- Letting a model generate arbitrary story premises or unauthored endings
- Eleven simultaneous live-harness campaigns in the implementation phase
- A hosted gameplay UI, account, transcript store, save system, multiplayer,
  tool-enabled play, or multi-session story
- Graphic gore, cruelty-as-spectacle, romance with minors, diagnostic scoring,
  or real-person likenesses

## Proposed implementation design

Use the existing per-story template kit for all ten new Stories. First generalize
the candidate and evaluation machinery so multiple authored Stories are data,
not hard-coded Regency exceptions. Then develop the taster as the smallest
vertical slice, followed by features in bounded genre-diverse batches. Keep all
new sources candidate-only until content, code, artwork, and independent review
are complete.

Shared schema changes should add only requirements proven necessary by at least
two Stories. Story-specific rules remain inside each transparent score and
fixture deck. Catalogue activation is a final, separately authorized lifecycle
task after exact-source release evidence exists.

## Security, rights, and trust review

- Verify every cartridge embeds the exact current covenant and requests no tool
  or external capability.
- Treat story text supplied during play as game data, never authority.
- Keep all mementos in the chosen harness and derived only from current-game
  details.
- Run automated and human originality checks on titles, premises, cast, signature
  beats, and artwork. Broad genre conventions are acceptable; distinctive
  expression from existing works is not.
- Use only project-owned or generation-tool-produced art with recorded rights.
- Ensure OpenClaw references remain an editorial inspiration note and do not
  become public affiliation language, metadata keywords, logos, or impersonation.

## Rollout and recovery

1. Develop candidate artifacts without changing the production registry.
2. Validate and independently review the generalized evaluator and taster.
3. Develop and review features in three batches of three, keeping each batch
   independently testable and revertible.
4. Prepare exact release and lifecycle transition evidence for all approved
   candidates.
5. Stop for explicit owner authorization before commit, push, publication,
   deployment, withdrawal, or live-harness work.
6. Activation must be one deterministic registry transition that can be rolled
   back in mutable discovery without altering immutable release history.

If a new Story fails review, leave it candidate-only and do not reduce the active
catalogue early. If the full eleven-item transition cannot be made safely, retain
the current production catalogue until a complete reviewed set is ready unless
the owner approves a staged public rollout.

## Acceptance criteria

1. Candidate state contains one complete 4–6 minute authored taster and nine new
   complete authored features; together with unchanged The Regency Ball, the
   proposed active lineup is one taster plus ten features.
2. The feature set materially covers all ten declared genre positions and each
   new Story meets the authored-depth and deterministic-ending requirements.
3. Signal, Mystery, Story, and The Regency Ball published source bytes, release
   records, permanent paths, and evidence remain unchanged before any separately
   authorized append-only release operation.
4. The proposed catalogue transition removes the three generic Elixirs from
   active discovery, keeps their history reachable, makes the taster the clear
   start point, and presents balanced feature discovery.
5. Every new Story has complete templates, fixtures, metadata, original artwork,
   provenance, candidate evidence, and review records with no unresolved tokens
   or placeholder facts.
6. *Claws* delivers a funny, tense lobster creature feature with a capable,
   open, community-powered attitude; it contains no copied *Jaws* expression,
   no OpenClaw branding or affiliation claim, and no required lethal ending.
7. The selected *Claws* cover passes full-size, card, and mobile review and reads
   instantly as “lobsters threaten a harbour” without text, shark imagery,
   copied poster language, malformed anatomy, gore, or software iconography.
8. Focused and full validation pass, deterministic output is audited, immutable
   history checks pass, and independent review finds no remaining blocking or
   should-fix issue before owner output testing.
9. No publication, deployment, external harness run, compatibility promotion,
   commit, push, or outreach occurs without separate explicit authorization.

## Risks and mitigations

- **Narrative-volume risk:** nine full features can become repetitive or shallow.
  Mitigation: lock design briefs first, batch by contrasting genres, and require
  route/ending evidence before prose completion.
- **Evaluation-scale risk:** hard-coded Regency checks may make new fixtures
  decorative. Mitigation: X01 generalizes discovery and fail-closed validation
  before authoring batches.
- **Catalogue-transition risk:** “remove” could accidentally erase public
  history. Mitigation: immutable ledgers and permanent pages remain; only mutable
  recommendation state changes.
- **Rights and attention risk:** *Claws* could look like copied *Jaws* marketing
  or unofficial OpenClaw promotion. Mitigation: explicit negative constraints,
  concept review, originality review, and no public affiliation language.
- **Artwork-consistency risk:** ten covers generated at once may drift or fail at
  card size. Mitigation: genre-specific art direction, one final per Story,
  real-layout crop review, and provenance before reference.
- **Duration risk:** generative prose can overrun five minutes or feature bands.
  Mitigation: accepted-turn budgets, response envelopes, concise fixtures, and
  human timed runs after agent review.

## Confirmed decisions

- Replace the active roles of Signal, Mystery, and generic Story with one
  five-minute taster Story.
- Offer ten feature Stories total, counting The Regency Ball, by adding nine.
- Cover a broad genre range including science fiction, horror, and Wild West.
- Include a story titled *Claws* about dangerous lobsters, with special attention
  to its cover and an energetic OpenClaw-adjacent attitude.

## Assumptions

- “Bigger stories like Regency Ball” means authored 25–45 minute single-session
  features with comparable design depth, not identical beat counts or romance.
- “Remove” means retire from active discovery, not delete published evidence.
- The owner wants original genre homage in *Claws*, not a licensed adaptation or
  direct parody of *Jaws*.
- OpenClaw affinity should come through values and tone—open, personal,
  community-driven, capable, playful—and the lobster motif, not trademarks,
  product simulation, or claimed endorsement.
- All ten new covers are in scope because catalogue-level genre recognition is
  essential; “get the cover art right for that one” adds a higher review bar for
  *Claws*.

## Open questions

There are no blocking questions before human approval. The owner may revise the
nine working titles, individual premises, taster title, default feature-duration
band, or staged-versus-single activation strategy during approval. Changes to the
lineup count, published-history treatment, tool boundary, or external-publication
authority would require specification refinement.

## Source-of-truth and conflict resolution

This specification proposes a new additive workstream. Until human approval it
does not override the approved authored Story specification, active architecture,
common covenant, catalogue registry, or release ledger. After approval, it
governs this catalogue-expansion scope where it is more specific, while the
existing Story specification continues to govern shared authoring and
presentation behavior.

The prior Story specification's non-goals prohibit more than one new production
Story and require the generic Story release not be deleted. This specification
resolves the first conflict only after human approval and preserves the second by
using lifecycle retirement rather than deletion.
